Federal programs

Federal

Canada Disability Benefit (CDB)

A federal, income-tested benefit for low- and moderate-income working-age people with disabilities.

It provides up to $204.20 per month (July 2026 to June 2027). DTC qualification is required. It is available to people aged 18 to 64. Payments began in July 2025.

Starting in fall 2026, recipients also receive a one-time $150 payment to help with the cost of applying for the DTC. No application is needed.

Federal

Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP)

A federal program, administered through Sun Life, that provides dental coverage to eligible Canadian residents of all ages who do not have access to dental insurance and whose adjusted family net income is under $90,000. Covered services include:

  • Preventive and diagnostic care
  • Restorative procedures
  • Endodontic and periodontal services
  • Prosthodontic services
  • Oral surgery

Cosmetic dentistry, such as teeth whitening, is not covered.

Information and application Full details: Canadian Dental Care Plan

Federal

Canada Pension Plan Disability (CPPD)

Financial assistance for people who are unable to work because of a severe and prolonged disability.

  • Eligibility: a severe, prolonged medical condition that prevents you from working, and contributions to the CPP during your working years.
  • Application: you must submit medical evidence of your disability and show that you cannot work.
  • Amount: depends on how much you contributed to the CPP.

CPPD is not paid past age 64.

Information and application Full details: CPP Disability

Federal

Registered Disability Savings Plan (RDSP)

A long-term savings plan for Canadians with disabilities. It includes:

  • Canada Disability Savings Grant (CDSG): government matching of up to 300% on personal contributions, to a maximum of $3,500 a year ($10,500 a year if retroactive amounts are available) and $70,000 over a lifetime.
  • Canada Disability Savings Bond (CDSB): the government contributes up to $1,000 a year, even with no personal contributions, to a $20,000 lifetime maximum. In 2026, the full $1,000 is paid when adjusted family net income is $38,237 or less, and a partial Bond is paid up to $58,523.

The Bond and Grant are paid until the end of the year the beneficiary turns 49.

Information and application Full details: Registered Disability Savings Plan

Federal tax credit

Disability Amount transfer

If the person who qualifies for the Disability Tax Credit (DTC) does not have enough taxable income to use the full disability amount, the unused part can be transferred to a supporting family member, such as a spouse, parent, or other relative.

For the 2026 tax year, the federal disability amount is $10,341, which can reduce federal tax by up to about $1,448 (provinces and territories add their own amount). To use the full credit themselves, a person generally needs about $26,800 or more in taxable income (the basic personal amount of $16,452 plus the disability amount).

It may also be possible to transfer the disability amount retroactively if it was not fully claimed in previous years.

Full details: Disability Tax Credit

Insured program · BC and Ontario only

Guaranteed Access Program (GAP)

The Guaranteed Access Program (GAP) provides a one-time, tax-free payout to a person's loved ones when they pass away.

It is available to individuals who hold a valid Disability Tax Credit (DTC) certificate or who receive benefits through a federal or provincial disability support program. It is offered through a Canadian insurer.

Participants contribute a small amount each month in return for a one-time lump-sum payment (minimum $10,000) to the person of their choosing.
