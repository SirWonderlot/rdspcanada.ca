British Columbia disability programs

Provincial supports for people living with disabilities in British Columbia.

Persons with Disabilities (PWD) Assistance

PWD status is a legal designation that lets eligible individuals receive:

  • Disability assistance (financial support)
  • Health supplements, such as dental care, medical supplies, and equipment
  • Transportation support, such as the BC Bus Pass
  • Earnings exemptions, so you can earn income without losing your full benefits

For a single person, PWD Assistance pays up to $1,483.50 a month for basic needs and shelter.

PWD Assistance: information and application

Community Living BC (CLBC)

A provincial Crown corporation that funds supports for people 19 or older who have a developmental disability.

CLBC also funds supports for adults diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) or Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorder (FASD) who need significant help with day-to-day tasks.

CLBC: information and application

At Home Program

Support for children and youth with severe disabilities and complex health care needs, including medical equipment and services for eligible children.

At Home Program: information and application

BC Bus Pass Program

A reduced-cost annual bus pass for eligible low-income seniors and people receiving disability assistance. Valid on BC Transit and TransLink services across the province.

BC Bus Pass: information and application

Guaranteed Access Program (GAP)

The Guaranteed Access Program (GAP) provides a one-time, tax-free payout to a person's loved ones when they pass away.

It is available to individuals who hold a valid Disability Tax Credit (DTC) certificate or who receive benefits through a federal or provincial disability support program. It is offered through a Canadian insurer.

Participants contribute a small amount each month in return for a one-time lump-sum payment (minimum $10,000) to the person of their choosing.

GAP: information and application

Home Renovation Tax Credit

Helps eligible people with the cost of certain permanent home renovations that improve accessibility, functionality, or mobility at home for people over 65 or people with a disability.

Information and how to claim

Property Tax Deferment Program

A loan program for eligible B.C. homeowners that lets you defer your current year's property taxes.

Information and application

Angling Licence Fee Reduction Program

B.C. residents with a severe and permanent physical or mental disability can get an annual Non-Tidal Angling Licence at a reduced fee.

Application (PDF)

Social Services Camping Fee Exemption

Lets people with disabilities who receive income assistance camp at frontcountry BC Parks campgrounds without paying the base camping fee.

It is available to people enrolled in programs such as Persons with Disabilities (PWD) benefits, the At Home Program, or Indigenous Services Canada Disability Income Assistance.

PharmaCare BC

PharmaCare has 12 plans that help B.C. residents pay for prescription medications, medical devices and supplies, and pharmacy services. One person can be covered by several plans.

Fair PharmaCare

Helps B.C. residents pay for many prescription drugs, dispensing fees, and some medical devices and supplies. Coverage is based on income: the less you earn, the more help you get.

First Nations Health Benefits (Plan W)

100% coverage of many prescription drugs and dispensing fees, some medical devices and supplies, and certain over-the-counter drugs and health products. Funded by the First Nations Health Authority.

Income Assistance (Plan C)

100% coverage of eligible prescription costs for B.C. residents receiving income assistance through the Ministry of Social Development and Poverty Reduction (SDPR), or in the care of or in an agreement with the Ministry of Children and Family Development (MCFD). SDPR and MCFD send eligibility information to PharmaCare, so you do not need to apply.

Psychiatric Medications (Plan G)

100% coverage of certain psychiatric medications for B.C. residents of any age with clinical and financial need (for example, annual income below $42,000).

Long-term Care (Plan B)

100% coverage of eligible prescription drugs and medical supplies for permanent residents of licensed long-term care facilities registered with Plan B. Not all facilities are registered, so you may want to ask before moving in (or moving a loved one in).

Cystic Fibrosis (Plan D)

100% coverage of eligible digestive enzymes in the Plan D formulary, and partial or full coverage of other products depending on your other PharmaCare plan. You must be registered with a provincial cystic fibrosis clinic, which arranges coverage.

Children in the At Home Program (Plan F)

100% coverage of eligible prescription drugs and designated medical supplies for children and teens with a severe disability or complex health care needs who receive benefits through MCFD's At Home Program. MCFD submits your information to PharmaCare.

Palliative Care (Plan P)

100% coverage of eligible palliative care medications in the Plan P formulary for B.C. residents of any age at the end stage of a life-threatening illness who wish to receive palliative care at home.

Assurance Plan (Plan Z)

100% coverage for any B.C. resident with Medical Services Plan (MSP) coverage. Plan Z currently covers contraceptives and Mifegymiso, opioid use disorder treatment (OAT), and Medical Assistance in Dying (MAiD) medications. Present your prescription and your Personal Health Number at a pharmacy and pay $0.

Smoking Cessation (Plan S)

100% coverage of nicotine gum, lozenges, or patches, and some or all of the cost of certain prescription smoking cessation drugs, for B.C. residents of any age.

BC Centre for Excellence in HIV/AIDS (Plan X)

Covers antiretroviral drugs for HIV-positive individuals through the BC Centre for Excellence in HIV/AIDS Drug Treatment Program.

Medication Management (Plan M)

Covers eligible medication management services provided by pharmacies, such as clinical services, medication reviews, and publicly funded vaccinations.

Assistive devices and disability support

Assistive devices

Assistive devices are “items that ease the strains of daily activities at home, at work, or at play. They include medical equipment, mobility aids, information technologies, practical aids, and gadgets to suit many different needs.” (Health Canada)

Governments offer a wide range of programs and services for people with disabilities, with total funding of more than $5 billion a year.

Support for people with disabilities

Many provincial and federal government agencies and non-profit organizations in Canada provide support to people with disabilities.
