By the numbers

The programs

Several federal and provincial disability benefits programs are available to people living with long-term medical conditions. They include the following:

  • Canada Disability Benefit (CDB): monthly income payments for working-age adults (up to $2,450 a year). This benefit can be paid retroactively.
  • Canadian Dental Care Plan (CDCP): dental care for people without dental insurance (no cost when family income is under $70,000).
  • Canada Disability Savings Bond (CDSB): free deposits into an RDSP, with no deposits of your own needed (up to $20,000). Receive up to $11,000 in deposits in your first year. Amounts can be paid retroactively.
  • Canada Disability Savings Grant (CDSG): the government matches what you deposit into an RDSP (up to $70,000). Amounts can be paid retroactively.
  • Canada Pension Plan Disability (CPP-D): monthly income payments for people who have worked and paid into the CPP (up to $20,894 in 2026). Amounts can be paid retroactively.
  • Guaranteed Access Program (GAP): a one-time, tax-free lump-sum payout, in BC and Ontario (minimum $10,000).
  • Disability Amount: a tax credit for annual refunds that can be transferred to a family member (up to $1,448 off federal tax in 2026). It can also be collected retroactively.
  • Provincial benefits programs: programs in each province and territory.

A person must be eligible to collect each benefit.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

If I qualify for a disability benefit, do you help with applying?

In many cases, yes, depending on the program. If you would rather apply yourself, direct links to each application on the government websites are provided.

Can I collect retroactive benefits?

Often, yes. The Canada Disability Benefit, the Canada Disability Savings Bond and the Canada Disability Savings Grant can all be paid retroactively. The Disability Amount can also be claimed for past years when it wasn't claimed, which can give a retroactive tax refund from the CRA. These are some examples.

Will I be asked for personal information?

No. The assessment tool does not ask for your name, address, SIN or birthdate. If you are eligible for a program, you are given a direct link to its official application.

What if I'm not eligible for one of the programs?

The Benefits Assessment Tool will tell you why. If there's a way you could become eligible, it will show you what to do next.
